## Clean VO text for TTS

```
Click.

You put your headphones on so nobody talks to you.

But your open office was never built for talking.

It started in nineteen-oh-six. In a soap company.

Frank Lloyd Wright built them one great open hall.

With balconies above.

From up there, every desk could be seen.

Every clerk — in sight. Every pause — noticed.

Nineteen sixty-eight. Robert Propst tries to give you walls.

Panels. Privacy. Room to move.

Companies packed his panels into boxes.

Smaller. Cheaper. Endless.

He later called the result "monolithic insanity."

So the walls came down again.

They called it collaboration.

In twenty eighteen, Harvard researchers measured what happened.

Face-to-face conversation fell by about seventy percent.

Everyone put on headphones.

A hundred and twenty years. Different shapes.

The same balcony.

You're not antisocial. You're building the wall they took away.

Click.
```

## Fact-check (still open from the long script)

- [ ] Larkin Administration Building, Buffalo, Frank Lloyd Wright, completed 1906; central light
      court with balconies.
- [ ] Robert Propst, Herman Miller, Action Office (II), 1968.
- [ ] Propst: "The cubiclizing of people in modern corporations is monolithic insanity." Confirm
      wording and year (reported late 1990s).
- [ ] Bernstein & Turban (2018), Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B: face-to-face interaction fell ~70% after
      a move to open-plan. Authors are from Harvard (Business School).
- [ ] "A hundred and twenty years" = 1906 to 2026.
