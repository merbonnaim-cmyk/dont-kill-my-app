# voice.md

Inferred from one video: "The Man Who Invented Your Productivity Tracker" (3:18, first upload).
One video is a thin sample. Revise after videos two and three.

## Who I am talking to

One person: an office or remote worker whose employer runs a tracker on their laptop. They have
jiggled the mouse to stay "active" and they feel guilty stepping away for coffee. They have never
heard of Frederick Taylor.

## How I actually talk

- Narrated history, voiceover only, no face on camera. Illustrated (anime-style) visuals.
- Short declarative sentences. Often fragments: "By one man." "On purpose."
- Second person, present tense, to pull the past into the viewer's chair: "You're sitting at your
  laptop..."
- Triads with dashes: "Every motion — broken down. Every second — recorded. Every pause —
  eliminated."
- Before/after pairs: "Before, a craftsman decided... Now, the man with the stopwatch decided."
- Turn lines that raise the stakes: "But that was only the surface." "But the worst was yet to come."
- Years and numbers written out as spoken words ("Eighteen ninety-nine", "forty-seven").
- A sound motif that opens and closes the video ("Click.").
- Real quotes from primary sources, quoted exactly.
- Ends on the viewer, not the history: a reframe that takes guilt off them ("You're not lazy.").

Sample lines:

> Click. That sound has been running your life for over a hundred years.
> He wasn't a worker. He was the son of a wealthy Philadelphia lawyer.
> Even his rest came on command.
> A hundred years have passed. The stopwatch never left.

## Words I do use

stopwatch, system, idle, the man with the stopwatch, "on command", "never left".

## What I will not claim

Only dated, sourced historical facts (Taylor, Midvale, Bethlehem Steel, Watertown Arsenal, Ford,
Leffingwell). Quote sources word for word. No invented statistics about modern trackers.

## My format

About 3-4 minutes. Cold open, no intro. Chronological story, then a jump to the present, then a
closing line that echoes the opening sound. English voiceover. Thumbnail: illustrated scene with
three words or fewer in bold caps, one word highlighted in yellow.
